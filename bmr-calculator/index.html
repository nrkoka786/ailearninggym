Understanding BMR and TDEE

Your Basal Metabolic Rate (BMR) is the number of calories your body burns just to stay alive — breathing, keeping your heart beating, regulating temperature, and running all your organs. Even if you stayed in bed all day doing nothing, you'd still burn this many calories.

Your Total Daily Energy Expenditure (TDEE) is your BMR multiplied by how active you are. It's the total number of calories you actually burn in a day, including exercise and everyday movement.

How to use your TDEE to reach your goals

  • To lose weight — eat 300–500 calories below your TDEE. A deficit of 500 calories per day leads to roughly 1 pound of fat loss per week.
  • To maintain your weight — eat at your TDEE. This is your maintenance level.
  • To gain muscle or weight — eat 200–500 calories above your TDEE (a "surplus"), ideally combined with strength training.

Which formula does this calculator use?

This calculator uses the Mifflin-St Jeor equation, which is considered the most accurate formula for estimating BMR in most adults. It was published in 1990 and is recommended by the American Dietetic Association. The formulas are:

  • Men: BMR = (10 × weight in kg) + (6.25 × height in cm) − (5 × age) + 5
  • Women: BMR = (10 × weight in kg) + (6.25 × height in cm) − (5 × age) − 161

Frequently asked questions

How accurate is this calculator?

The Mifflin-St Jeor formula is accurate to within about 10% for most adults. Individual variations in metabolism, muscle mass, genetics, and hormones mean no formula is perfectly precise. Use it as a starting point, then adjust based on real-world results over 2–4 weeks.

Why does my TDEE seem too high or too low?

Activity level is the biggest variable. Many people overestimate how active they are — "moderately active" means dedicated exercise sessions, not just walking to your car. If your results don't match your experience, try a lower activity multiplier.

Does BMR change over time?

Yes. BMR tends to decrease as you age, partly due to natural muscle loss. Building and maintaining muscle through strength training is one of the most effective ways to keep your metabolic rate higher as you get older.

Is this the same as counting calories?

Your TDEE tells you the target — how many calories to aim for. Calorie counting is the process of tracking what you actually eat and comparing it to that target. Together, they form the basis of most evidence-based nutrition approaches for weight management.
