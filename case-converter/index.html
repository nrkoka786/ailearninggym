About This Tool

This case converter lets you change the capitalization of any text with one click. Paste your text, pick a format, and the result appears instantly in the same box — ready to copy.

What each format does

  • UPPERCASE — converts every letter to capitals. Useful for headings, labels, or emphasis.
  • lowercase — converts every letter to small. Useful for cleaning up text typed with caps lock on.
  • Sentence case — capitalizes only the first letter of each sentence. Good for fixing copy-pasted text that lost its formatting.
  • Title Case — capitalizes the first letter of each major word. Standard for article headlines, book titles, and headings.
  • Capitalize Words — capitalizes the first letter of every word, including small words like "the" and "and". Useful for names and lists.

When would you use this?

  • Someone sent you text in ALL CAPS and you want to make it readable.
  • You're formatting a headline and need consistent title case.
  • You're cleaning up data exported from a spreadsheet or database.
  • You need to match a specific capitalization style for a document or website.

Title Case vs. Capitalize Words — what's the difference?

Title Case follows standard editorial rules — it skips capitalizing small connecting words like "a", "the", "and", "of", and "in" when they appear in the middle of a title. Capitalize Words capitalizes every single word without exception. For most article or blog headlines, Title Case is the conventional choice.

Frequently asked questions

Is my text stored anywhere?

No. Everything runs in your browser. Your text is never sent to any server and disappears when you close the tab.

Does it handle special characters and emojis?

Yes — the converter only changes the case of letters and leaves everything else (punctuation, numbers, emojis, symbols) untouched.

Can I convert very long documents?

Yes. There's no character limit — paste as much text as you need and it converts instantly.

Does it work for non-English text?

It works for most Latin-script languages (French, Spanish, German, etc.). For languages without a case distinction (like Chinese, Japanese, or Arabic), the text will pass through unchanged.
