25 ChatGPT Prompts for Teachers: Lesson Plans, Quizzes & Grading (Copy-Paste)

📅 Published October 2026 | ⏱ 10 min read

These ChatGPT prompts for teachers are built for one purpose: giving you hours back every week. Lesson planning, quiz writing, feedback drafting, parent emails — the repetitive writing that eats evenings and weekends is exactly what AI drafts fastest. Every prompt below is copy-paste ready: replace the bracketed parts with your specifics, and always give it your grade level.

A quick note on doing this right: AI drafts, you decide. Review everything before it reaches students — AI can get facts wrong, and your professional judgment is the quality control. With that rule in place, these 25 prompts will change your Sundays.

ChatGPT Prompts for Teachers: Lesson Planning

1. "Create a 45-minute lesson plan for 7th grade science on the water cycle. Include an opening hook, direct instruction outline, one hands-on activity, and a 5-minute exit ticket."

2. "Give me 3 engaging opening activities for a high school history lesson on the Great Depression. Each should take under 10 minutes."

3. "Outline a week-long unit plan for 4th grade fractions. One learning goal per day, with a short activity for each."

4. "Suggest 5 real-world examples I can use to teach supply and demand to 10th graders who find economics boring."

5. "Turn this topic into a project-based learning assignment with a rubric: [paste your topic]."

ChatGPT Prompts for Teachers: Quizzes and Assessments

6. "Write a 10-question multiple-choice quiz on the American Revolution for 8th grade. Include an answer key."

7. "Create 5 open-ended discussion questions about 'To Kill a Mockingbird' that push 9th graders beyond plot summary."

8. "Generate a vocabulary quiz with 12 words from our genetics unit, with definitions and one example sentence each."

9. "Make a true/false quiz on internet safety for 5th graders, with explanations for each answer."

10. "Draft clear instructions for a take-home essay assignment, including length, due date placeholder, and grading criteria."

Grading and Feedback Prompts

11. "Give me 10 constructive comment starters I can use when grading essays that need major revision."

12. "Rewrite this feedback to sound encouraging but honest: [paste your comment]."

13. "Create a simple rubric for a 6th grade science poster project: 4 criteria, 4 performance levels each."

14. "Suggest 5 specific praise phrases for students who improved but still aren't at grade level."

Parent Communication Prompts

15. "Draft a friendly email to parents introducing myself at the start of the school year. Warm tone, under 200 words."

16. "Write a sensitive email to a parent about their child's missing assignments. Firm but kind, and invite a meeting."

Differentiation and Support Prompts

19. "Give me 4 scaffolding strategies for English learners in a 9th grade biology class."

Admin Time-Saver Prompts

23. "Write a field trip permission slip letter to parents with all standard sections."

How to Get Good Results From These Prompts

Three habits separate great results from mediocre ones. First, always replace the bracketed parts — "7th grade science" and "water cycle" aren't decoration; specificity is what makes the output usable. Second, state the grade level every time, even when it feels obvious — a 5th-grade explanation and a 10th-grade explanation are different documents. Third, ask for revisions: "make it shorter," "simpler language," "add an example." The first draft is a starting point, not a final product — for AI and for lesson planning alike.

Adapting These Prompts to Your Subject

The 25 ChatGPT prompts for teachers above are written generically so anyone can use them — but they get dramatically better when tuned to your subject. Here are three quick adaptations:

Math teacher: Take prompt #1 and add "include one common misconception students have about the water cycle and how to address it" — becomes "...about solving two-step equations. Include the common mistake of forgetting to do the same operation to both sides, and a 2-minute mini-lesson correcting it." Misconception-targeting is where AI lesson help shines in math.

Art teacher: Prompt #13 (rubrics) becomes: "Create a rubric for a 7th grade perspective-drawing project: criteria for technique, creativity, effort, and presentation, 4 levels each, with student-friendly language." AI is surprisingly good at rubric language when you name the criteria.

PE / special subjects: Prompt #25 (policy summaries) becomes: "Turn these gym safety rules into a fun 3-minute talk for 3rd graders: [paste rules]." Anything you repeat verbally every term is worth generating once and reusing forever.

The pattern: keep the prompt's structure, swap in your subject's vocabulary, and add one subject-specific instruction (a misconception, a technique, a safety rule). Thirty seconds of customization, ten times the relevance.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is it okay for teachers to use ChatGPT for planning?

Yes — as a planning assistant, not a replacement for your judgment. Districts increasingly accept AI-assisted prep; always review outputs before classroom use and follow your school's AI policy.

Will ChatGPT write biased or incorrect lesson content?

It can make factual errors, especially with dates, statistics, and scientific claims. Verify anything factual before teaching it. Think of AI as a fast first-draft writer with an occasionally unreliable memory.

Can students tell if I used AI to plan?

It doesn't matter if you review and adapt — the lesson that reaches students carries your professional judgment, your knowledge of your kids, and your adjustments. That's what makes it yours.

Is the free version of ChatGPT enough for teachers?

Yes — every prompt above works on the free tier. Consider paid only if you hit usage limits during heavy planning periods like the start of a term.
