How to Write AI Image Prompts for Beginners: The 7-Part Formula (+ Examples)

📅 Published October 2026 | ⏱ 9 min read

If you want to learn how to write AI image prompts for beginners, here's the core insight: bad AI images almost always come from short prompts. Type "a dog" into DALL-E, Midjourney, or Gemini and you'll get the most average dog imaginable — because when you don't specify, the AI fills in the blanks with the most ordinary choices available. Detail is control. This guide gives you a simple 7-part formula that turns vague ideas into images you'll actually want to keep.

Why "A Dog" Gives You a Boring Picture

AI image generators don't imagine — they predict. Given your words, they produce the most statistically likely image matching them. "A dog" matches millions of generic dog photos, so you get the visual equivalent of elevator music.

Compare:

Weak: "a dog in a park."

Strong: "A golden retriever puppy running through a sunlit meadow, ears flying, shallow depth of field, warm golden-hour light, photorealistic, highly detailed."

Same idea, wildly different result. The strong version works because every added detail removes one decision the AI would otherwise make averagely. That's the whole game: the more good decisions you make in words, the fewer average ones the AI makes for you.

How to Write AI Image Prompts for Beginners: The 7-Part Formula

You don't need all seven every time — four or five strong parts beat one vague sentence. But knowing all seven gives you a checklist:

  1. Subject. Who or what is in the image. Be specific: not "a woman" but "an elderly woman with silver hair and laugh lines."
  2. Action. What it's doing. "Running," "reading by a window," "pouring coffee" — action creates life.
  3. Setting. Where it happens. "Sunlit meadow," "rainy Tokyo street at night," "cozy cabin interior."
  4. Style or medium. Photorealistic, oil painting, anime, 3D render, watercolor, cyberpunk. Without this, you get the model's default look.
  5. Lighting. Golden hour, soft studio light, moonlit, neon glow. Lighting is the fastest way to change a mood.
  6. Composition and camera. Close-up portrait, wide landscape shot, shallow depth of field, aerial view. This controls framing like a photographer.
  7. Mood and quality tags. "Cozy," "dramatic," "serene" — plus finishers like "highly detailed" or "4k" that nudge overall quality.

5 Worked Examples (Weak → Strong)

Portrait. Weak: "a man." Strong: "Close-up portrait of a fisherman in his 60s, weathered face, gray stubble, soft window light, shallow depth of field, photorealistic, highly detailed."

What changed: age, texture, light, and camera all specified.

Cozy interior. Weak: "a living room." Strong: "Cozy living room interior at dusk, crackling fireplace, warm lamp glow, bookshelf wall, knitted throw on armchair, cinematic lighting, photorealistic."

What changed: time of day, light sources, and specific objects.

Fantasy landscape. Weak: "a castle." Strong: "Ancient stone castle on a cliff above a misty sea, dramatic storm clouds, moonlight breaking through, epic fantasy matte painting style, atmospheric, highly detailed."

What changed: setting, weather, light, and an explicit art style.

Product photo. Weak: "a coffee mug." Strong: "Matte black ceramic coffee mug with steam rising, on a rustic wooden table, soft morning window light, blurred café background, professional product photography, shallow depth of field."

What changed: material, props, lighting, and photography style.

Anime character. Weak: "anime girl." Strong: "Anime-style illustration of a cheerful girl with short teal hair and round glasses, holding a stack of books, cherry blossoms drifting, soft pastel colors, studio Ghibli inspired, detailed."

What changed: appearance specifics, action, setting details, and a style reference.

4 Beginner Mistakes That Ruin Image Prompts

  1. Too few words. Under ~10 words, you're letting the AI decide everything. Aim for 2-4 sentences.
  2. Contradictory instructions. "Dark but bright and cheerful" confuses the model — pick one mood.
  3. Forgetting the style. No style word means the model's default, which is usually bland photorealism.
  4. Not iterating. The pros generate, look, add one detail, and regenerate. Your third version beats your first every time.

Adapting the Formula to Different Art Styles

The 7-part formula works everywhere, but different styles reward different parts. Here's how to weight it:

For photorealism, camera and lighting terms do the heaviest lifting. Words like "85mm lens," "shallow depth of field," "golden hour," and "candid" push the model toward believable photography. Example: "Candid street photo of an elderly man feeding pigeons, overcast daylight, 35mm lens, shallow depth of field, photorealistic."

For anime and illustration, style references matter most. Name the look: "studio Ghibli inspired," "90s anime cel-shaded," "flat vector illustration." The model has strong associations with named styles, so one good style phrase can replace three generic adjectives.

For abstract or digital art, mood and color carry the image. "Melancholic," "vibrant neon palette," "flowing organic shapes" — here you're directing feeling more than objects. Example: "Abstract digital artwork of ocean waves at dawn, flowing organic shapes, teal and coral palette, dreamlike, highly detailed."

The pattern: decide what matters most for your style, spend your words there, and let the formula's other parts play supporting roles. Two well-weighted sentences beat six scattered ones.

How to Write AI Image Prompts for Beginners: Your First 5-Minute Exercise

Ready to put how to write AI image prompts for beginners into practice? Do this right now: open any free image generator (ChatGPT, Gemini, or Firefly), and generate the same idea three times. Round 1: type just the subject ("a lighthouse"). Round 2: add setting, style, and lighting ("a lighthouse on rocky cliffs at sunset, dramatic clouds, photorealistic"). Round 3: use the full 7-part formula with action, composition, and mood. Compare the three side by side. The jump from round 1 to round 3 — from generic to gallery-worthy — is the entire lesson of this guide in five minutes. Save your round-3 prompt; it's your personal template for everything after.

Frequently Asked Questions

Which AI image tool should beginners start with?

Start wherever you already are: ChatGPT, Gemini, and Adobe Firefly all generate images on free tiers. The 7-part formula transfers between tools, so learning it once pays off everywhere.

How long should an image prompt be?

The sweet spot is roughly 30-60 words — two to four sentences. Shorter than that and you're under-specifying; much longer and you risk diluting the key details.

Can I use AI images commercially?

It depends on the tool's terms of service — most paid tiers allow commercial use, free tiers vary. Check your specific tool's terms before selling anything with AI images in it.

Why do hands and faces sometimes look weird?

Hands, teeth, and small text are the classic weak spots of image models. Mitigate by adding "detailed hands" to your prompt, generating at higher quality settings, or simply regenerating — each run is a new roll of the dice.
