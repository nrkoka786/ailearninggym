About This Tool

This tip calculator makes it easy to figure out how much to tip at a restaurant and how to split the bill evenly among a group. Enter the bill amount, choose a tip percentage, and set how many people are splitting — the totals update instantly.

How to use it

  1. Enter the bill amount — type in your total check (before or after tax, your choice).
  2. Set the tip percentage — drag the slider to your desired tip. 15% is standard, 18–20% is common for good service, 25% for exceptional.
  3. Enter the number of people — the calculator divides everything evenly and shows each person's share.

What tip percentage should you leave?

Tipping customs vary by country and type of service, but here are common US guidelines:

  • 15% — adequate service at a sit-down restaurant
  • 18–20% — good service, the most common range
  • 25%+ — exceptional service or a way to show extra appreciation
  • 10% or less — poor service (though many still tip 15% minimum)
  • Takeout — tipping is optional; 10–15% is appreciated
  • Delivery — 15–20% is standard, more in bad weather

Should you tip on the pre-tax or post-tax amount?

Most etiquette guides suggest tipping on the pre-tax subtotal since you're tipping for the service, not the government tax. That said, many people tip on the post-tax total for simplicity — the difference is usually only a dollar or two either way.

Tips for splitting bills fairly

Splitting a restaurant bill evenly works well when everyone ordered similar items. If one person had significantly more (or less) than others, it helps to itemize first and then split the tip evenly. Most people find a clean round-up easier — for example, if the calculator says $23.47 each, everyone putting in $24 leaves a little extra and avoids coin-counting.

Frequently asked questions

Does this tool store my data?

No. Everything runs in your browser. No data is sent to any server — your bill amounts and calculations stay completely private on your device.

Can I use this without an internet connection?

Yes — once the page loads, the calculator works entirely offline. Handy if you're at a restaurant with poor signal.

What if the bill doesn't split evenly?

The calculator rounds to the nearest cent. In practice, just round everyone up to the nearest dollar — it's simpler and the extra cents go to a slightly better tip.

Is a mandatory service charge the same as a tip?

No. Some restaurants automatically add a gratuity (usually 18–20%) for large groups. Check your bill first — if a service charge is already included, you don't need to add another tip on top unless you want to.

How much should I tip for bad service?

Many people still tip 10–15% even for poor service, since servers often share tips with kitchen staff and busboys who had nothing to do with the problem. If the service was genuinely bad, speaking to a manager is usually more effective than leaving nothing.
