About This Tool

This word counter tracks words, characters, paragraphs, and estimated reading time as you type. It's useful for essays, blog posts, social media captions, or any writing with a length limit. Everything runs in your browser — your text is never sent anywhere.

How to use it

  1. Type or paste your text into the box above.
  2. The counts update automatically as you type — no button needed.
  3. Use Copy Text to copy your text to the clipboard, or Clear to start fresh.

Common word count targets

Here are typical length guidelines for different types of writing:

  • Tweet / X post — 280 characters max
  • Instagram caption — 125 words recommended, 2,200 max
  • Email subject line — 6–10 words for best open rates
  • Blog post (short) — 500–800 words
  • Blog post (standard) — 1,000–1,500 words
  • Long-form SEO article — 1,500–2,500 words
  • College essay — 500–650 words (Common App)
  • Short story — 1,000–7,500 words
  • Novel — 70,000–100,000 words

How reading time is calculated

The estimated reading time is based on an average reading speed of 200 words per minute — a widely used benchmark for general adult readers. A 1,000-word article takes about 5 minutes to read at this pace. Academic or technical content may take longer; simple content may be faster.

Frequently asked questions

Does this tool save my text?

No. Your text stays in your browser only. Nothing is uploaded or stored. Closing the tab or refreshing the page clears everything.

Why does my word count differ from Microsoft Word?

Different tools handle hyphenated words, numbers, and punctuation slightly differently. For example, "real-time" might count as one word or two depending on the tool. For most purposes the difference is minimal — usually just 1–3 words.

Does it count characters with or without spaces?

The character count shown includes spaces. If you need a count without spaces, subtract the number of words minus one as a rough approximation, or use a dedicated character counter.

Can I use this for non-English text?

Yes — the character count works for any language. However, word counting relies on spaces between words, so it works best for languages that separate words with spaces (most European languages). Languages like Chinese or Japanese without spaces will show inaccurate word counts.
